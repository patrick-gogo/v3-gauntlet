01 · The idea

Plan by day. Build overnight. Push on your word.

You plan a ticket with Claude on the laptop. A devbox lead builds, reviews and tests it while you sleep. In the morning you answer a few questions and say "push".

On your laptop

  • Planning, rulings and your answers
  • The Notion queue board and Jira
  • Push day: the push, the PR, the chat draft
  • Every token (GitHub, Notion, chat)

On the devbox

  • Building test-first, one commit per task
  • Fresh reviewers and critics, the gates, the browser checks
  • The final self-review and the handoff
  • No tokens, no plugins: rules travel as files

02Words you'll see

Lap
One /v3-lap: one devbox session for a set of tickets, from packing until push day. It has one id and its tickets keep their branches for the whole lap.e.g. lap-20261006-0944, branch devbox/lap-20261006-0944/bugfix/ABC-123-...
Round
One build, review and test pass inside a lap, ending in a handoff. Round 1 is the first night.
Follow-up
A message to the same devbox session that starts the next round: round 2, round 3. It comes from your handoff answers or from the push-day review. A finished session still takes follow-ups.
New lap
Only when you send new tickets with /v3-lap. A new round is never a new lap.
Wave
Every ready branch merged into a throwaway branch and deployed to the stack once.
Run
One pass of the gates and the browser checks on a wave.
Exit pair
Two green runs in a row on the same build.
Handoff
The lead's note: questions with its picks, a ticket table (Ready or Needs you), evidence, rulings, follow-ups, and the review-request fields.
Push day
The laptop step after you say "push": checks, push, draft PR, Jira comment, chat draft. Nothing reaches GitHub before it.

03The flow, step by step

Each step says who does it. you means you act, Claude means the laptop session works on its own, devbox means the lead or its helpers.

Day · your laptop
  1. InboxyouNotion
    • Add the ticket key to the Gauntlet Queue board, column Inbox.
    • /v3-ticket with no argument lists the Inbox to pick from.
  2. PlanClaudeLaptop
    • Reads the ticket from Jira, translates any Japanese, moves your To Do ticket to In Progress.
    • Code context, an unattended brainstorm, then a plan with a test for every task.
    • Every open choice becomes a numbered ruling with its pick, tagged T1, T2 or T3. Nothing waits for you.
    • Writes the ticket, design, plan and rulings onto the Notion card, and your own ticket notes.
    Out: acceptance criteria, plan, rulings, scope, gates, budget
  3. Rulings batchyouLaptop
    • One message: every ruling's pick, the alternative and the cost if wrong, T3 and T2 first.
    • A changed ruling updates the plan; only changed rulings come back.
    1 keep 2 change: reuse the existing modal 3-5 keep
  4. Queue ityouNotion
    • The card is Planned. Drag it to Queued and set its Order (lower runs first).
  5. Start the lapyouLaptop to devbox
    • /v3-lap packs the Queued tickets from a clean copy of master, so your uncommitted edits never leave the laptop.
    • It sends the rules, the brief and each ticket's plan, starts the lead, and marks the cards Running. Close the laptop.
Night · devbox, unattended
  1. Freeze the basedevbox
    • Brings the stack up from the clean base and runs every gate there first. Failures on the base are known reds and never count against a ticket.
  2. Builddevbox
    • Each ticket on its own branch. Per task: a failing test first, the smallest fix, one commit, a fresh reviewer.
    • One heavy thing at a time; anything over 10 minutes runs detached.
  3. Ticket reviewdevbox
    • Fresh critics on the whole ticket: impact, security and regression, requirements, project conventions.
    • Only fix-here findings are fixed, test first. The rest go to the handoff as follow-ups.
  4. Wave, run, triagedevbox
    • Ready branches merged and deployed together, then the gates and browser checks. A product bug goes back to Build for that ticket only.
  5. Exit pairdevbox
    • Two green runs in a row on the same build end the round. A stop time keeps it from starting new tickets near morning.
  6. Final self-reviewdevbox
    • Four lenses (conventions, correctness, history, in-file comments) plus an impact trace, written in the format of your own pre-PR review, with the reviewed commit.
  7. Handoffdevbox
    • Questions with picks (T2 and T3 rulings are always questions), the ticket table, evidence, follow-ups and the review-request fields.
    Out: one branch per ticket, the handoff on the job's branch
Morning · your laptop
  1. ResultyouLaptop
    • /v3-lap result fetches the branches, moves each card to Ready or Needs you, puts the handoff and final review on the card, and shows the questions.
  2. AnsweryouLaptop to devbox
    • One line answers every question. A "change" goes to the same session as a follow-up.
    1 keep 2 change: keep the old label 3 yes
    A change starts round 2: back to Build, same lap
  3. Push dayyou say "push"Laptop
    • Safety check (clean base, right author and committer, no tool attribution), secret scan, and how far master has moved.
    • Uses the devbox's final review; runs a review first if it is missing or the code changed.
    • Pushes from a temporary copy, never your main checkout. Opens the draft PR, comments on Jira, drafts the chat review request, moves the card to PR open.
    A problem found here also starts a round, before anything is pushed
  4. MergedClaude
    • After the merge, /v3-ticket <id> marks the ticket done and cleans up the branch. The card moves to Done.

04Lap vs round

One lap can have several rounds. Here is a real one, ticket key replaced.

Lap lap-20261006-0944one devbox session · one branch: bugfix/ABC-123-...
Round 1 · night

Built and Ready

Test-first build, review, gates, handoff. You answered "1 keep 2 yes".

Round 2 · follow-up

Push day stopped

The push-day review found master had merged a new tax rule after round 1 started. The fix went to the same session: merge master, change one formula, update the tests.

Round 3 · only if needed

Another follow-up

If the spec owner had ruled differently, one more follow-up to the same session. Still the same lap.

Rule of thumb: new tickets mean a new lap; changes to tickets already built mean a new round. A round never pushes. GitHub only sees a branch on push day, after you say "push".

  1. The stack and the baseline are paid for once per lap, not once per ticket.
  2. Each ticket is built on its own branch from the same clean master, one at a time.
  3. A wave merges every reviewed branch and deploys it: it catches tickets that pass alone but clash together.
  4. A product bug goes back to that ticket only, then a new wave.
  5. Two green runs in a row, a final self-review per Ready ticket, then one handoff for all three.

A ticket not started by the stop time comes back as Needs you. Budgets are per ticket, so one hard ticket cannot eat the others' share.

07Rules that still decide

Ruling levels

T1An obvious technical choice. The loop decides.
T2A choice the shop would make (required or optional, limits, defaults, wording). Goes ahead with a pick, always a question for you before push.
T3A business decision (a new feature, money, tax, legal). Flagged for a full talk with the spec owner.

Unsure between two levels? Take the higher one.

House rules inside the devbox

  • One heavy thing at a time: builds, the browser checks and the backend tests take turns.
  • Anything over 10 minutes runs detached.
  • Unclear? Write a ruling and keep going. Never ask, never wait, never push.
  • No tool attribution in any commit.

What stays on the laptop

  • The GitHub, Notion and chat tokens. The devbox has none, so the board updates in the morning.
  • Your uncommitted edits: laps are packed from a clean copy.
  • Company details live in a private config, never in this public repo.
